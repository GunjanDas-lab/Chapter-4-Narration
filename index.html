Direct and Indirect Speech

Definition: Direct Speech is when we report someone’s exact words, often enclosed in quotation marks. Indirect Speech (or reported speech) conveys the meaning of what someone said without quoting their exact words.

Three Kinds of Sentences

1. Assertive Sentences 📢

Direct Speech: An assertive sentence states a fact or opinion.

Example: She said, “I am studying for my exams.”

Indirect Speech: In indirect speech, the structure changes slightly. The reporting verb (said) remains the same, but the sentence is modified to convey the meaning without quotes.

Example: She said that she was studying for her exams.

Changes in Assertive Sentences:

  • Change in pronouns: “I” becomes “she,” etc.
  • Change in tense: Present simple to past simple (e.g., “am studying” to “was studying”).
  • The conjunction “that” is often added.
I➜sheam studying➜was studying+ that

2. Interrogative Sentences ❓

Direct Speech: An interrogative sentence asks a question.

Example: He asked, “Are you coming to the party?”

Indirect Speech: When converting to indirect speech, we usually change the question format to a statement.

Example: He asked if I was coming to the party.

Changes in Interrogative Sentences:

  • The reporting verb changes from “asked” to “inquired” or “wanted to know.”
  • The question format is altered to fit a statement.
  • The pronouns and tenses may change similarly to assertive sentences.
Are you coming?➜ifIwas coming

3. Exclamatory Sentences 🤩

Direct Speech: An exclamatory sentence expresses strong emotion.

Example: She exclaimed, “What a beautiful day!”

Indirect Speech: In indirect speech, exclamations are reported with phrases that convey the emotion without quotes.

Example: She exclaimed that it was a beautiful day.

Changes in Exclamatory Sentences:

  • The reporting verb often changes to “exclaimed” or “cried.”
  • The exclamatory expression is converted into a statement.
  • The phrase may include “how” or “what” to convey the intensity.
